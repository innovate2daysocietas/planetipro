Get real NASA data for your site

This generator writes a ready-to-run Google Earth Engine script for any place. It samples NASA vegetation (MODIS), rainfall (GPM IMERG), soil moisture (SMAP) and slope (SRTM) and exports the exact CSV PLANETI needs. Earth Engine is free for research and nonprofit use.

  1. Enter your site’s centre and its half-width, or use the selected site above.
  2. Copy the script into code.earthengine.google.com and click Run.
  3. Open the Tasks tab, click Run on planeti_data, and wait for the CSV in Google Drive.
  4. Open the CSV, copy its contents, and paste them into the box above.

The script uses MODIS NDVI for 2005 and 2024 (the last full year), IMERG monthly rainfall for 2020–2024, SMAP surface soil moisture for 2024, and SRTM slope. Dataset names change over time, so if Earth Engine reports a missing dataset, search its catalog for the new version.

What PLANETI is

PLANETI is a restoration planning tool for communities, district officers and NGOs. It answers three questions with NASA open data: what did we lose, where should we act first, and is it working? The name comes from the Luganda word for planet, Planeti, and it is built for the NASA Space Apps 2026 theme, The Next Frontier, which includes problems in our own backyard.

The problem

Uganda’s wetlands, forests and hillsides hold water, protect soil and support farming. Clearing and drainage cause floods, silted rivers, water shortages and failed harvests. Planting trees alone is not enough: planting in the wrong place wastes seedlings, and nobody sees the loss until it is too late. PLANETI is inspired by QUEÑARIS, the Peruvian team that won the 2025 Local Impact Award for protecting queñua forests that feed Arequipa’s water.

How it works

1. See the past

Yearly vegetation index (NDVI) from Landsat or MODIS, 2005 to today, shows where cover has gone and when.

2. Rank the places

Each grid square gets a score from vegetation lost, rainfall, soil moisture and slope. You set the weights to match your goal.

3. Track recovery

New satellite passes update the trend. A sudden NDVI drop in one year raises a new clearing alert.

Score formula. For each square, score = (wL·loss + wR·rain + wS·soil + wP·slope) ÷ (wL + wR + wS + wP), where every input is scaled 0 to 1 and the w values are the sliders. Squares with under 10% vegetation loss score zero, since there is nothing to restore. Higher means plant sooner. Steep squares with high loss also get advice on erosion control.

Alert rule. A square raises an alert when its NDVI falls by more than 0.10 compared with the previous year.

Going from prototype to real data

  1. Draw the boundary of your site in Google Earth Engine (free for research and nonprofit use) or NASA Earthdata AppEEARS.
  2. Create yearly NDVI composites for 2005 and the latest year, plus annual rainfall, mean soil moisture and slope.
  3. Sample them on a regular grid, around 100 m to 1 km apart, and export as CSV with the columns above.
  4. Paste the CSV into the tool above. Share the ranked list with the district or community group.

Who benefits

Farmers and households who depend on stable water, district environment officers who choose planting sites, youth groups and schools running nurseries, and NGOs who need evidence for funding. Every ranked site comes with a plain-language reason, so non-specialists can act on it.

Honest limits

The map above uses simulated data to demonstrate the workflow, not real measurements for these places. It cannot yet pull live NASA data by itself, because browsers block that from a static page. NDVI shows greenness, not tree species, so teams should confirm sites on the ground. Rankings are decision support, not a substitute for local knowledge.

Three-minute pitch

Problem (30 s): name the site and show a before and after. Data (45 s): show the 20-year trend and the NASA datasets. Demo (60 s): move the year slider, change the weights, click the top site. Impact (30 s): say who acts on it. Next steps (15 s): name your partners.
